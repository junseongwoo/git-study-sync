Article: Brindle Foods
Brindle Foods Extends Reusable-Container Trial
Brindle Foods will continue its reusable lunch-container trial for another six months after reporting a
strong response from customers at its two downtown shops. Since January, customers have been
able to borrow a container by paying a $4 deposit and recover the deposit when they return it to
either participating shop. No purchase is required at the time of return.
The company originally intended to assess the project in April. However, a temporary closure of one
shop in February reduced the amount of data available. Managing director Ravi Desai said that an
extended trial would provide a more reliable picture of how frequently containers were reused.
Staff clean and inspect returned containers before placing them back into circulation. Containers
with damaged lids are removed, even if their lower sections remain usable. Customers are asked to
return all parts together, although a missing lid does not prevent them from receiving their deposit
back.
The company plans to publish the trial results in October. It has not yet decided whether to
introduce the scheme at its suburban shops. Desai noted that those locations have different
patterns of customer visits, and the company would need to consider those differences before
expanding the program.

Report: Northgate Shuttle
Northgate Business Park Shuttle: Trial Review
The four-week shuttle trial between Northgate Station and the business park ended on June 28. The
service carried 1,240 passengers in total. Use was highest between 7:30 and 9:00 a.m., while the
final evening departure at 7:00 p.m. attracted an average of only three passengers.
The passenger survey drew 186 responses. Most respondents valued the direct route, but several
requested a stop at the medical center. Adding that stop would increase the journey by
approximately eight minutes and require a change to the published timetable. We recommend
keeping the direct route for the next phase and investigating a separate midday service to the
medical center.
Operating costs were within the approved trial budget. However, one driver’s absence required a
replacement bus from a contractor on two days. The contractor’s vehicle had fewer seats, and
passengers on one morning departure had to wait for the next bus. A reserve-driver arrangement
should be established before regular service begins.
The report does not recommend committing to a year-round contract yet. Instead, a second trial
during the autumn would help determine whether passenger demand is affected by seasonal
working patterns. The business park committee will review this recommendation at its July meeting.
